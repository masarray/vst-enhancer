Stereo enhancer VST3 workflow

Make a mix feel wider and deeper without treating width as a one-knob race.

ArSonKuPik can be used as a musical stereo enhancer on tracks, buses or a full mix. The useful goal is not maximum width: it is a clearer sense of space while the centre, low end and mono playback remain believable.

Download and evaluateRun a fair A/B test
Goal

Width is only useful when the mix still translates

  • Listen for more separation and depth around a stable centre, not simply a larger left-right image.
  • Use the Stereo control gradually and judge the result on familiar material.
  • For bass-heavy music, pay special attention to whether the foundation becomes vague or disconnected.
Starting workflow

Start from balance, then add space

  • Begin with Signature Balanced or another preset that already fits the tonal direction.
  • Increase Stereo only until the presentation opens up; compensate other controls only when the mix actually needs it.
  • Use Gain Match when deciding whether the spatial change is genuinely better rather than simply louder.
Verification

Check mono, centre focus and low-end stability

  • Collapse the result to mono in your DAW or monitoring chain and listen for important elements that become unexpectedly weak.
  • Use your normal correlation, phase and loudness meters when the release requires technical verification.
  • Compare at multiple listening levels; exaggerated width can sound exciting at first and tiring later.
Boundary

This is not a dedicated stereo-analysis suite

  • ArSonKuPik is an enhancer, not a replacement for a vectorscope, correlation meter or forensic phase tool.
  • It does not promise that every source will tolerate the same amount of stereo processing.
  • For mastering delivery, keep the final decision tied to mono compatibility and the destination format.
Evidence before hype

Use your own mix as the test signal.

ArSonKuPik includes Gain Match specifically so a spatial change can be judged at a more comparable level. The public measurement and A/B guides explain how to separate loudness, tone and translation when evaluating the result.
